Sharepoint Migration Best Practices

Discover proven SharePoint migration best practices to ensure seamless transition, minimize downtime, and maximize user adoption in your organization.

Understanding Your Current Environment

Before embarking on any SharePoint migration journey, it's essential to conduct a thorough assessment of your current environment. This foundation work will inform your entire migration strategy and help you avoid common pitfalls that can derail even the most well-planned projects.

Start by inventorying your existing SharePoint sites, libraries, lists, and custom solutions. Many organizations discover content they didn't know existed or haven't accessed in years. This discovery phase is crucial for determining what truly needs to migrate versus what can be archived or decommissioned. Tools like SharePoint Migration Assessment Tool (SMAT) can help automate this process and provide detailed reports on your current implementation.

Analyze your custom code, workflows, and third-party integrations. These often represent the most complex migration challenges. Document how each custom solution works, what business processes it supports, and whether equivalent functionality exists in the target environment. This analysis will help you decide whether to migrate, rebuild, or replace each custom component.

Planning Your Migration Strategy

A successful SharePoint migration requires careful planning and stakeholder alignment. Begin by establishing clear objectives for the migration project. Are you moving to SharePoint Online for better collaboration, improved security, cost savings, or to enable modern features like Microsoft Copilot? Your objectives should drive technical decisions and help prioritize migration activities.

Develop a detailed migration timeline that includes all major phases: assessment, planning, pilot, migration, validation, and post-migration optimization. Build in contingency time for unexpected issues, as migrations rarely proceed exactly according to plan. Consider conducting a phased migration rather than attempting a "big bang" approach, which allows you to learn and adapt your strategy based on early experiences.

Identify and prioritize your migration scope. Not all content should migrate with equal priority. Focus first on business-critical sites and content that support core operations. Establish criteria for prioritization based on factors like usage frequency, business impact, and technical complexity. This prioritization ensures that your most important content is migrated early when the team has the most energy and resources available.

Choosing the Right Migration Tools

The tools you select for your SharePoint migration can significantly impact project success and timeline. Microsoft provides several migration tools, each designed for different scenarios and requirements. SharePoint Migration Tool (SPMT) is Microsoft's free tool for basic migrations and works well for straightforward scenarios involving standard SharePoint content.

For more complex migrations involving large volumes of content, custom metadata, or specialized requirements, consider third-party migration tools. Solutions like ShareGate, AvePoint, and Metalogix offer advanced features for mapping permissions, preserving version history, and handling custom content types. These tools often include reporting capabilities that help track migration progress and identify issues before they become problems.

Evaluate migration tools based on your specific requirements rather than just features or cost. Consider factors like the volume of content, complexity of custom solutions, available timeline, and team expertise. Many organizations benefit from a hybrid approach, using SPMT for straightforward migrations and specialized tools for complex scenarios.

Data Preparation and Cleanup

One of the most valuable but often overlooked aspects of SharePoint migration is the opportunity to clean up and optimize your content. Migration represents a unique chance to address years of content accumulation, inconsistent naming conventions, and outdated information that can clutter your new environment.

Implement a content governance framework before migration to establish standards for site structure, metadata, naming conventions, and retention policies. Work with business stakeholders to identify content that should be archived, deleted, or consolidated. This cleanup effort will reduce migration volume and improve the quality of your target environment.

Address permission and access issues during the preparation phase. Many organizations discover permission inconsistencies, orphaned users, and excessive access rights during migrations. Use this opportunity to implement the principle of least privilege and establish clear access models for different types of content. This not only improves security but also simplifies ongoing administration.

Testing and Validation Strategy

Thorough testing is essential for ensuring migration success and user satisfaction. Develop a comprehensive testing plan that covers functional testing, performance testing, and user acceptance testing. Start with a pilot migration involving a representative sample of sites and content to validate your approach and identify any issues before the full migration.

Test all custom solutions, workflows, and integrations in the target environment before going live. Pay special attention to solutions that rely on deprecated features or on-premises dependencies, as these may require redesign or replacement. Validate that permissions and access controls work as expected and that users can perform their essential tasks without disruption.

Establish clear validation criteria for determining migration success. These criteria should include quantitative measures like migration completeness and performance metrics, as well as qualitative measures like user satisfaction and business process continuity. Document any issues encountered during testing and develop remediation plans before proceeding with the full migration.

Post-Migration Optimization

The migration project doesn't end when the last piece of content moves to the target environment. Post-migration activities are critical for ensuring long-term success and user adoption. Begin by conducting a thorough post-migration validation to confirm that all content migrated correctly and that users can access and work with their content as expected.

Provide comprehensive training and support for users to help them adapt to the new environment. Focus on the differences between the old and new environments, and highlight new features and capabilities that can improve productivity. Consider appointing power users as champions who can help their colleagues and provide feedback on the migration experience.

Monitor system performance and user adoption in the weeks and months following migration. Use analytics tools to track usage patterns, identify underutilized features, and detect any performance issues. Continuously optimize the environment based on this feedback, making adjustments to site structures, permissions, and configurations to better meet user needs.
