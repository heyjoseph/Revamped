SharePoint Records Management Guide

Implement SharePoint records management to ensure regulatory compliance, automate retention, and manage your organization's information lifecycle effectively.

Understanding Records Management Requirements

Records management ensures that your organization's information is properly managed throughout its lifecycle to meet legal, regulatory, and business requirements. SharePoint provides comprehensive records management capabilities that can significantly streamline compliance efforts.

Assess your organization's specific records management requirements based on industry regulations and business needs. Different industries have different requirements for record retention, disposition, and accessibility. Understanding these requirements is essential for designing an effective SharePoint records management solution.

Define what constitutes a record in your organization and when documents transition from active documents to records. This definition should reflect your compliance requirements and business practices. Clear record definitions ensure consistent application of records management policies across your organization.

Implementing In-Place Records Management

SharePoint's in-place records management allows you to manage records within their existing collaboration context rather than moving them to separate repositories. This approach maintains accessibility while applying appropriate records management controls.

Configure in-place records management in SharePoint to declare records without moving them from their original location. This approach allows users to continue collaborating on documents until they're declared records, then applies appropriate restrictions and retention policies automatically.

Implement retention policies that automate record lifecycle management. SharePoint retention policies can automatically declare records based on document properties or events, apply retention periods based on record types, and trigger disposition reviews when retention periods expire. These automations reduce manual effort while ensuring compliance.

Configuring Compliance and Security

Records management requires strong compliance and security controls to ensure records remain authentic, accessible, and secure throughout their lifecycle. SharePoint provides comprehensive capabilities for meeting these requirements.

Implement information protection policies that classify and protect records based on sensitivity. Use sensitivity labels to apply appropriate protection to records, including encryption, access restrictions, and usage policies. Ensure these protections maintain record accessibility for authorized users while preventing inappropriate access.

Configure audit logging for all records management activities. SharePoint provides comprehensive audit capabilities that track record declaration, access, modifications, and disposition. Ensure audit logs are retained for sufficient periods to meet compliance requirements and support investigations.

Designing Record Center Architecture

For organizations with extensive records management requirements, a dedicated record center provides centralized management and governance. A well-designed record center architecture supports efficient records administration while maintaining accessibility.

Design a record center structure that aligns with your record types and retention requirements. Create separate libraries or folders for different record categories, retention schedules, or business functions. This structure facilitates efficient records administration and ensures appropriate retention policies are applied.

Implement routing mechanisms that automatically move records to the record center based on predefined criteria. SharePoint can automatically route records to the record center when they're declared or based on document properties. This automation ensures consistent records management while reducing manual effort.

Managing Disposition and Destruction

Record disposition and destruction represent critical phases of the records lifecycle. Proper disposition processes ensure records are retained for required periods and destroyed appropriately when retention expires.

Configure disposition review processes to validate destruction decisions. SharePoint can require manual review before records are destroyed, allowing stakeholders to confirm that records can be safely deleted. This review process provides accountability and ensures destruction decisions are appropriate.

Implement secure destruction processes that permanently delete records when disposition is approved. Ensure destruction processes are logged and that records are completely removed from all systems. Test destruction processes to verify they completely remove data and cannot be recovered.

Training and User Adoption

User adoption is critical for records management success. Even well-designed records management systems fail if users don't understand their responsibilities or follow proper procedures.

Provide comprehensive training for users on records management policies and procedures. Training should cover when to declare records, how to declare them, what restrictions apply to records, and how to access records when needed. Tailor training to different user roles based on their records management responsibilities.

Establish clear guidance and support resources for records management questions. Create user guides, FAQs, and support channels where users can get help with records management. Consider appointing records management liaisons in each department who can provide guidance and ensure compliance.
